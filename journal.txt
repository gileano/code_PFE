PROPOSED STRUCTURE
=====================================================================

1. Introduction
   - Motivation for surveying QIEA-for-MOO, including the energy-
     efficiency/sustainability rationale (banciu2026aiot, sai2026generative
     -- see "LITERATURE" above).
   - Scope and inclusion criteria for the literature covered.
   - Contributions of this survey.
   - Paper organization.

2. Background
   - Quantum computing concepts needed for QIEA (qubit-representation
     encoding of solutions, e.g. Han & Kim-style Q-bit chromosomes,
     rotation-gate update rules).
   - MOO fundamentals: Pareto dominance, hypervolume, IGD, spread/spacing.
   - Classical MOEAs used as the baseline QIEA methods are compared
     against: NSGA-II, NSGA-III, SMS-EMOA, SPEA2, MOEA/D.

3. Taxonomy
   - Classification axes for QIEA-MOO methods: representation scheme,
     quantum-inspired operator type (rotation gate vs. interference vs.
     entanglement-inspired), selection/diversity mechanism, hybridization
     with classical MOEAs, application domain.

4. Feature comparison
   - Systematic table across taxonomy categories: representation,
     complexity, scalability, convergence properties, benchmark problems
     used, reported performance.

5. Critical analysis / limitations
   - Per-category weaknesses.
   - Cross-cutting open problems: reproducibility gaps, weak benchmarking
     practices, scalability claims not empirically stress-tested.

6. Case study: block-partitioned quantum circuit optimization
   - Framed as a direct extension of ghlib2026scalable (see "LITERATURE"
     above) -- lead with what M1_finale/ adds on top of
     that baseline (NSGA-III and SMS-EMOA as additional per-block
     optimizers, plus the inter-block injection and compression stages)
     before using the pipeline to ground abstract taxonomy claims in a
     concrete worked example.
   - Candidate grounding points (pending re-verification on the fixed
     injection-stage code before being cited -- see logs.txt):
     - NSGA-II vs. SMS-EMOA vs. NSGA-III as a worked example of
       crowding-distance vs. hypervolume-driven vs. reference-point
       selection trade-offs.
     - "No universally-best mutation operator, not stable across scale"
       as a concrete instance of a taxonomy/limitations claim.
     - Hypervolume comparability across algorithms being metric-sensitive
       (adaptive vs. fair/shared-reference HV) as an illustration of the
       benchmarking-practice limitations raised in section 5.
   - Framed explicitly as illustrative, not as this paper's primary
     evidence -- full empirical validation deferred to the planned
     empirical results paper.

7. Open challenges and future directions

8. Conclusion
