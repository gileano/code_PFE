\title{Critical Analysis of Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithms\\
for Multi-Objective Optimization:\\
Taxonomy, Features, and Limitations}
\author{Olivian-Antonio G\^{\i}lea \and Adrian Florea}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) borrow the Q-bit
chromosome and rotation-gate operator from quantum mechanics to improve
classical evolutionary search. Their multi-objective optimization (MOO)
branch, active since at least 2006, remains sparse, scattered across
domains, and, to our knowledge, unsurveyed on its own terms. A
bibliometric analysis of 2{,}250 QIEA papers from 1996--2026 shows
multi-objective work making up under a tenth of the field, with its
share roughly doubling since 2024, and clustering with application
domains rather than with algorithm design. We propose a taxonomy of
QIEA-for-MOO methods along five axes (representation, operator type,
selection mechanism, hybridization, and application domain), apply it
in a feature comparison that uses single-objective QIEAs as a baseline, and critically analyze the
field's limitations. Across thirteen methods selected by citation,
selection mechanisms are varied but never reference-point- or
hypervolume-based, several highly cited methods do not state how they
combine objectives, and methods are rarely benchmarked against one
another. The rotation gate's reference solution, obvious with one
objective, changes most with several, yet only three methods document
their choice.
Reproducibility gaps, weak benchmarking, and scalability claims tested
at a single problem size recur across method categories. An
illustrative case study, a block-partitioned quantum-circuit-optimization
pipeline extending a published NSGA-II framework with NSGA-III and
SMS-EMOA, shows how an adaptive, per-run hypervolume reference point can
change a cross-algorithm ranking and how a correctness bug can silently
invalidate weeks of results. We close with open challenges, including
the untested combination of a multi-objective QIEA with a
quantum-computing problem domain.
\end{abstract}
