\Title{Critical Analysis of Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithms for
Multi-Objective Optimization: Taxonomy, Features, and Limitations}

\corres{Correspondence: olivianantonio.gilea@ulbsibiu.ro}

\abstract{Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) use Q-bit
chromosome representations and quantum rotation-gate operators to
improve the search efficiency and diversity of classical evolutionary
algorithms. Their multi-objective optimization (MOO) branch, active
since at least 2003, remains sparse and has no dedicated survey. A bibliometric analysis of 2{,}250 QIEA
papers from 1996--2026 shows multi-objective work making up under a
tenth of the field, its share roughly doubling since 2024. This
work proposes a five-axis taxonomy of QIEA-for-MOO methods (solution
representation, operator type, selection mechanism, hybridization, and
application domain), applies it in a feature comparison with
single-objective QIEAs as a baseline, and critically analyzes the
field's limitations. Selection mechanisms vary widely, yet none is
reference-point- or hypervolume-based; several widely cited methods do
not state how they aggregate objectives, and systematic benchmarking is
scarce. An illustrative case study, a block-partitioned
quantum-circuit-optimization pipeline extended with NSGA-III and
SMS-EMOA, shows how an adaptive hypervolume reference point can change
a cross-algorithm ranking. Two hypothetical application scenarios, a
chemical plant and an industrial microgrid, show that these limitations
bite hardest on mixed-variable, constrained, and expensive-to-evaluate
problems. Finally, since no multi-objective QIEA has yet been applied to
a quantum-computing problem, the paper outlines future directions,
including ongoing work in FADSE.}

\keyword{quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms; multi-objective
optimization; Q-bit representation; taxonomy; bibliometric analysis;
NSGA-II; NSGA-III; hypervolume; quantum circuit optimization}
