\begin{abstract}
By integrating core principles of quantum mechanics---specifically
Q-bit chromosome representations and quantum rotation-gate
operators---quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) enhance the
search efficiency and diversity of classical evolutionary paradigms. Their multi-objective optimization (MOO)
branch, active since at least 2003, remains sparse, scattered across
domains, and, to the best of the authors' knowledge, has not yet been
the subject of a dedicated survey. A
bibliometric analysis of 2{,}250 QIEA papers from 1996--2026 shows
multi-objective work making up under a tenth of the field, with its
share roughly doubling since 2024, and clustering with application
domains rather than with algorithm design. This work proposes a taxonomy of
QIEA-for-MOO methods along five axes (solution representation, operator type,
selection mechanism, hybridization, and application domain), apply it
in a feature comparison that uses single-objective QIEAs as a baseline, and critically analyze the
field's limitations. A review of the most-cited and most recent
multi-objective QIEA methods reveals significant diversity in selection
mechanisms, none of which adopt reference-point or hypervolume-based
indicators. Furthermore, several widely cited approaches fail to clarify
how objectives are aggregated, and systematic comparative benchmarking
remains scarce. In particular, while defining the reference solution for
quantum rotation gates is straightforward in single-objective
optimization, multi-objective scenarios introduce substantial
complexity---a design choice explicitly documented by only three of
these methods.
Reproducibility gaps, weak benchmarking, and scalability claims tested
at a single problem size recur across method categories. An
illustrative case study, a block-partitioned quantum-circuit-optimization
pipeline extending a published NSGA-II framework with NSGA-III and
SMS-EMOA, shows how an adaptive, per-run hypervolume reference point can
change a cross-algorithm ranking and how a correctness bug can silently
invalidate weeks of results. Finally, building on the identified
gap---no multi-objective QIEA has yet been applied to a
quantum-computing problem domain---and on a synthesis of key features
in existing platforms, this paper outlines future research avenues and
details ongoing developments within FADSE (Framework for Automatic
Design Space Exploration), an open-source platform developed at Lucian
Blaga University of Sibiu. By providing a standardized infrastructure
for metaheuristic execution, benchmark interfacing, and performance
evaluation, FADSE enables the simulation of both multi-objective QIEA
variants incorporating adaptive quantum-inspired operators and
resource-efficient hybrid QIEA--memetic architectures.
\end{abstract}
