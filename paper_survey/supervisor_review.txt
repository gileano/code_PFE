33. (p.39) NSGA-II keeps the best individuals, so HV should not decrease;
    explain why it goes up and down.
   -> Old explanation (crowding distance trading convergence for diversity)
      replaced. Cause, from the code: each generation's HV is computed after
      normalizing by that generation's own population min/max, with a
      reference point derived from the same bounds (evaluate_run /
      normalize_objectives in final_m1_script.py). When the population's
      range widens, the front shrinks in normalized coordinates and HV drops
      even though the front has not deteriorated. Text notes that with a fixed
      reference point elitist NSGA-II's HV should not fall, apart from small
      losses from crowding-distance truncation. Figure caption now says
      "Adaptive (per-generation-normalized) hypervolume".
      Based on code inspection; not re-run with a fixed reference point.
