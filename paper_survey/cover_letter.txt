Subject: Submission of a review manuscript entitled "Critical Analysis of
Quantum-Inspired Evolutionary Algorithms for Multi-Objective Optimization:
Taxonomy, Features, and Limitations"

Dear Editor,

We are pleased to submit the above manuscript for consideration as a
Review article in Processes.

Quantum-inspired evolutionary algorithms (QIEAs) represent candidate
solutions as probabilistic Q-bit chromosomes updated by rotation-gate
operators. They are increasingly used to solve engineering optimization
problems on classical hardware. Most real process-design and operation
problems are multi-objective, but the multi-objective branch of the QIEA
literature has never been surveyed on its own. It has been active since at
least 2003, but it remains small and fragmented. Our manuscript addresses
this gap with four contributions:

  1. A bibliometric analysis of 2,250 QIEA papers (1996-2026) built from
     OpenAlex. Multi-objective work makes up under a tenth of the field,
     and its share has roughly doubled in recent years.
  2. A five-axis taxonomy of multi-objective QIEAs: solution
     representation, quantum-inspired operator type, selection mechanism,
     hybridization with classical multi-objective evolutionary algorithms,
     and application domain. We apply it in a systematic feature
     comparison, with single-objective QIEAs as a baseline.
  3. A critical analysis of the field's limitations. Selection mechanisms
     vary widely, but none is reference-point- or hypervolume-based.
     Several widely cited methods do not state how they aggregate their
     objectives, and systematic benchmarking is scarce.
  4. An illustrative case study: a block-partitioned
     quantum-circuit-optimization pipeline extended with NSGA-III and
     SMS-EMOA. On a 225-run experimental baseline, it shows how an
     adaptive hypervolume reference point can change a cross-algorithm
     ranking, which is a concrete benchmarking pitfall.

We believe the manuscript fits the scope of Processes, in particular its
"Process Control, Modeling and Optimization" and "Automation Control
Systems" (optimization algorithms) sections, and it is relevant to
"Energy Systems". The survey is intended as a guide for practitioners who
choose or design evolutionary optimizers for process problems. To connect
its findings directly to process engineering, Section 7 works through two
hypothetical but representative application scenarios: the multi-objective
design of a methanol plant and the 96-step dispatch of an industrial
microgrid. It shows where the identified limitations matter most: mixed
continuous/discrete variables, hard constraints, and expensive
evaluations. Finally, the manuscript outlines future research directions
within FADSE, an open-source framework for automatic design space
exploration with parallel evaluation on high-performance computing
systems.

To support reproducibility, the pipeline code, the experiment tooling, the
225-run dataset, and the cached bibliometric data are openly available, as
described in the Data Availability Statement.
